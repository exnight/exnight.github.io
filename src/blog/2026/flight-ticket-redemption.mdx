## TL;DR

Redeeming flight tickets can save you 35-50% on premium cabins if you plan ~1 year ahead. This is the 1st post of the series on the flight ticket redemption topic.

## What Is Flight Ticket Redemption

Apart from paying cash for flight tickets, a lot of airlines offer a way to redeem them with points. Airlines release limited seats for redemption and the number of points required varies based on the class, route, and season. Scarce business seats, limited premium economy (PEY) ones, and abundant economy seats are common. You may find more seats for regional flights than long-haul routes, mainly due to the number of flights and operation costs.

## Why I Care About It

As someone who travels a few times a year, I always buy the tickets. However, I found long-haul flights tiring as I could not sleep well. After paying for a PEY trip to London and business seats to Zurich, I realized that I couldn't go back to economy seats for those flights.

However, business class tickets are expensive for most cases. I'm listing a few round-trip examples below, checking ~3 months ahead at the time of writing (prices in HKD with equivalent USD avg):

| Route                           | Economy            | Premium Economy      | Business             |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------ | -------------------- | -------------------- |
| Hong Kong ⇄ London (one stop)   | ~5700-6600 ($790)  | ~17000 ($2180)       | ~25000-35000 ($3850) |
| Hong Kong ⇄ London (CX direct)  | ~7500-8100 ($1000) | ~18000 ($2310)       | ~50000-60000 ($7050) |
| Hong Kong ⇄ Zurich (direct)     | ~7600-8300 ($1020) | ~15000-16000 ($1990) | ~46000-48000 ($6030) |
| Hong Kong ⇄ New York (one stop) | ~8000-9000 ($1090) | ~20000-33000 ($3400) | ~43000-55000 ($6280) |
| Hong Kong ⇄ New York (direct)   | ~9100 ($1170)      | ~32000 ($4100)       | ~64000 ($8200)       |

It is hard to justify paying that much for those lie-flat seats, even though they are tempting. I later learned the alternative redemption method, did the math, and realized the potential savings.

## It's Not That Simple

Airline points can be earned by flying with the airline or its partners, or by transferring credit card points to the airline's loyalty program. A mileage or loyalty program is a reward system to give customers points or "miles" when one buys the services. Points cover ticket fares, but not taxes and fees. Getting the points is just the first step, seat availability and calculation are the harder parts.

### My Trips

Before I go into the details, let me share my experience so far. I had a trip to Switzerland in 2026 early July and I redeemed flight tickets in mid-July 2025, one year ahead. I will have a trip to the East Coast in 2027 and I just redeemed the tickets. Prices are in HKD with equivalent USD in brackets.

| Flight                 | Redemption Requirement | Cost (HKD) per Point | Equivalent Cash |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------- | -------------------- | --------------- |
| Hong Kong → Zurich     | 88000 miles + HKD934   | 0.108-0.120          | ~11000 ($1410)  |
| Zurich → Hong Kong     | 77877 miles + CHF356   | 0.122-0.137          | ~13700 ($1760)  |
| Hong Kong → East Coast | 80000 miles + HKD2239  | 0.108-0.120          | ~11400 ($1460)  |
| East Coast → Hong Kong | 119000 miles + HKD1808 | 0.108-0.120          | ~15400 ($1970)  |

_Taxes and fees are charged by mileage programs depending on the route, thus in multiple currencies. The overall equivalent cash values are normalized._

Comparing with the cash prices, I had a 35%+ discount for the Switzerland trip (back then it cost ~HKD38000) and a ~50% discount for the East Coast trip. The savings are big enough to justify my time and effort.

### The Concepts

Cash price = base fare + taxes and fees + points value. If one redeems a ticket, one won't get those frequent flyer points.

Therefore, redeem when the monetary and **time cost** of redemption is significantly lower than the total of base fare and frequent flyer points one would have earned. (I ignore the status points because I don't chase for airline membership.)

Take Cathay Pacific HKG-LHR as an example:

- A one-way economy ticket costs 27000 Asia Miles and HKD1787 (taxes and fees) to redeem. The equivalent cash is ~HKD4860 which is more expensive than half a round-trip (~3900), thus making no sense monetary because one gets frequent flyer miles (and status points) from cash tickets.
- A one-way business ticket costs 91000 Asia Miles and HKD1807 (taxes and fees) to redeem. The equivalent cash is ~HKD12200 which is ~44% of half a round-trip (~27500). I still need to pay money for the more comfortable experience, but not breaking my bank.

In general, if cash prices are high, it makes more sense and more beneficial to redeem tickets. Premium classes usually yield better redemption rates (maximizing value of the points).

## No Available Seats

One may say airlines release limited premium seats and too many people are competing for one. That is a brutal truth but also reasonable. From the economics perspective, redemption saves you money which means there are other **hidden costs** to balance and compensate, e.g., time, effort, and knowledge, etc.

Airlines offer redemption/"award" tickets to monetize unsold seats (points are valuable), build customer loyalty, and generate massive upfront revenue by selling points to banks/credit card companies. As selling tickets with cash prices creates more profit, airlines would release just enough "award" seats to not disappoint customers, but not too many so people don't pay cash prices anymore.

A typical release pattern:

- initial batch 330-360 days before departure
- rolling releases between 3-9 months before departure
- last minute release 14-30 days before departure

Seats.Aero provides a list of release dates for common airline mileage programs [here](https://seats.aero/tools/releases?source=all).

Therefore, one needs to plan ahead and catch the early-bird opportunities for popular/long-haul flights, as airlines release limited seats (good cash sales) and the routes are competitive (i.e., seats gone in a few minutes for HKG↔JFK and TPE↔JFK).

## Takeaways

In general, either redeem at initial release (330-360 days ahead), or at last minute (14-30 days before departure). Searching 3 months out is unlikely to yield good results.

Economy seats seldom justify the costs. Premium classes extract more value per point than their cash cost.

Don't ignore the hidden costs. Planning ahead, time spent monitoring, flexibility on dates and routes, and knowledge on release patterns and sweet spots are the "price" you pay instead of cash.
