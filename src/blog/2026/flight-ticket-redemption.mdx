## What means flight ticket redemption

Apart from paying cash for flight tickets, a lot of airlines offer a way to redeem them with points. Airlines release limited seats for redemption and the number of points required varies based on the class, route, and season. Scarce business seats, limited premium economy (PEY) ones, and abundant economy seats are common. You may find more seats for regional flights than long-haul routes, mainly due to the number of flights and operation costs.

## Why I care about it

As someone who travels a few times a year, I alway buy the tickets. However, I found long-haul flights tiring as I could not sleep well. After trying a PEY trip to London and paid business seats to Zurich, I realized that I couldn't go back to economy seats for those flights.

However, business class tickets are expensive for most cases. I'm listing a few round-trip examples below, checking ~3 months ahead at the time of writing (prices in HKD with equivalent USD avg):

| Route                               | Economy            | Premium Economy      | Business             |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------ | -------------------- | -------------------- |
| Hong Kong \<=\> London (one stop)   | ~5700-6600 ($790)  | ~17000 ($2180)       | ~25000-35000 ($3850) |
| Hong Kong \<=\> London (CX direct)  | ~7500-8100 ($1000) | ~18000 ($2310)       | ~50000-60000 ($7050) |
| Hong Kong \<=\> Zurich (direct)     | ~7600-8300 ($1020) | ~15000-16000 ($1990) | ~46000-48000 ($6030) |
| Hong Kong \<=\> New York (one stop) | ~8000-9000 ($1090) | ~20000-33000 ($3400) | ~43000-55000 ($6280) |
| Hong Kong \<=\> New York (direct)   | ~9100 ($1170)      | ~32000 ($4100)       | ~64000 ($8200)       |

It is hard to justify paying that much for those lie-flat seats, even though they are tempting. I later learned the alternative redemption method, did the math, and realized the potential savings.

## It's not that simple

Airline points can be earned by flying with the airline or its partners, or by transferring credit card points to the airline's loyalty program. Points cover ticket fares, but not taxes and fees. Getting the points is just the first step, seat availability and calculation are the harder parts.

### My Trips

Before I go into the details, let me share my experience so far. I had a trip to Switzerland in 2026 early July and I redeemed flight tickets in mid-July 2025, one year ahead. I will have a trip to the East Coast in 2027 and I just redeemed the tickets.

| Flight                   | Redemption Requirement | Cost per Point (HKD) | Equivalent HKD |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------- | -------------------- | -------------- |
| Hong Kong =\> Zurich     | 88000 miles + HKD934   | 0.108-0.120          | ~11000 ($1410) |
| Zurich =\> Hong Kong     | 77877 miles + CHF356   | 0.122-0.137          | ~13700 ($1760) |
| Hong Kong =\> East Coast | 80000 miles + HKD2239  | 0.108-0.120          | ~11400 ($1460) |
| East Coast =\> Hong Kong | 119000 miles + HKD1808 | 0.108-0.120          | ~15400 ($1970) |

Comparing with the cash prices, I had a 35%+ discount for the Switzerland trip (back then it cost ~HKD 38000) and a ~50% discount for the East Coast trip. The savings are big enough to justify my time and effort.

### The Concepts

Cash price = base fare + taxes and fees + points value. If you redeem a ticket, you won't get those frequent flyer points.

Therefore, redeem when the monetary and **time cost** of redemption is significantly lower than the total of base fare and frequent flyer points you would have earned. (I ignore the status points because I don't chase for airline membership.)
